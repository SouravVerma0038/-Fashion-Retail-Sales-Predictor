Neural Networks & Deep Learning Project Viva Reference

Academic and managerial concepts explaining the StyleSense architecture, training dynamics, and business trade-offs

1. Problem Statement & Business Objectives

Modern Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) fashion brands operate with compressed product lifecycles, volatile seasonal demand spikes (e.g. Diwali festive seasons, wedding calendars), and heavy reliance on paid performance marketing. The business objective of StyleSense is to accurately predict weekly sales units at the SKU-category level. Accurate forecasts minimize two costly retail errors:

  • Stockouts (Under-forecasting): Lost revenue, customer churn to competitors, and damaged brand equity.
  • Excess Inventory (Over-forecasting): High working-capital lockup, warehousing costs, and forced margin-diluting clearance markdowns.

2. Artificial Neural Network (ANN) Architecture

An Artificial Neural Network is composed of interconnected layers of artificial neurons:

  • Input Layer: In our model, 17 numerical and one-hot encoded features feed into the network: Month, 6 Category indicators, 3 Channel indicators, Price, Discount %, Ad Spend, Festival Season, Influencer Flag, Email Blast Flag, and Stock Level.
  • Weights & Biases: Each connection possesses a tunable weight \(W_{ij}\) representing signal strength, plus an adjustable bias scalar \(b\). The affine transformation is computed as \(z = \sum_{j} W_{ij} x_j + b\).
  • Hidden Layers: In ANN-1 we deploy one hidden layer of 32 neurons. In ANN-2 we deploy a deeper 2-layer hierarchy (64 → 32 neurons) that constructs higher-order feature representations (e.g., the synergistic non-linear interaction between Ethnic Wear, high discount, and festival season).
  • Output Layer: A single continuous scalar node representing predicted demand.

3. Forward Propagation & Activation Functions

During forward propagation, input feature tensors pass sequentially through affine transformations and activation functions:

  • Hidden Activation — ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit): Defined as \(f(z) = \max(0, z)\). ReLU introduces essential non-linearity into the network, allows sparse activations, and completely avoids the vanishing gradient problem inherent in sigmoid and tanh activations.
  • Output Activation — Linear Activation: The output layer uses a linear identity function \(f(z) = z\). Unlike classification networks which bound outputs to \([0, 1]\) via Softmax or Sigmoid, regression demand modeling requires an unbounded continuous real-valued range to predict sales volumes accurately.

4. Loss Function: Mean Squared Error (MSE)

The objective function optimized during training is the Mean Squared Error:

MSE = (1 / N) * ∑ (y_actual - y_predicted)²

In retail demand prediction, MSE is especially suitable because squaring the residual errors heavily penalizes large forecasting mistakes, forcing the neural network to avoid catastrophic stockout-level prediction errors.

5. Backpropagation, Gradient Descent & Adam Optimizer

Learning occurs via gradient-based optimization:

  • Backpropagation: Evaluates the partial derivative of the loss function with respect to every weight and bias using the mathematical chain rule of calculus (\(\frac{\partial L}{\partial W} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial z} \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial W}\)).
  • Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation): Instead of naive Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Adam maintains exponentially decaying averages of past gradients (first moment / momentum) and past squared gradients (second moment / RMSProp). This provides adaptive learning rates per parameter, enabling rapid and stable convergence through steep ravines and plateaus.

6. Regularization, Overfitting & Data Splits

Deep networks with many parameters risk memorizing training noise rather than learning underlying macroeconomic patterns:

  • Dropout (Rate = 0.2 in ANN-2): During each training mini-batch pass, 20% of neurons in the first hidden layer are randomly deactivated. This prevents co-adaptation of features, forcing the network to develop redundant, robust internal representations.
  • Early Stopping (Patience = 10): Monitors validation loss after every epoch. If validation loss does not achieve a new minimum for 10 consecutive epochs, training terminates automatically to prevent overfitting.
  • Three-Way Split & Zero Data Leakage: We partition into 68% Training (weight updates), 12% Validation (early stopping checkpointing), and 20% Held-out Test (final honest benchmark). Crucially, the Z-score normalizer (\(\mu, \sigma\)) is fitted strictly on training data and reused for validation, testing, and what-if predictions without look-ahead bias.

7. Why Compare Against a Linear Baseline?

In MBA analytics and executive AI governance, the principle of parsimony (Occam's Razor) is foundational. A complex neural network should never be deployed unless it demonstrably outperforms a simple, explainable linear model. Our baseline model implements Linear Regression (\(\text{Dense}(1, \text{linear})\)). The neural network justifies its added complexity because retail sales exhibit non-linearities: logarithmic diminishing returns of ad spend and non-linear discount elasticity that linear models cannot natively fit without manual polynomial feature engineering.

8. Evaluation Metrics Explained

  • MAE (Mean Absolute Error): \(\frac{1}{N}\sum |y - \hat{y}|\). Measures average magnitude of error in natural units (e.g., "our model is off by an average of 14 shirts per week"). Highly interpretable for merchandising managers.
  • RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error): \(\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum (y - \hat{y})^2}\). Penalizes large outlier forecast errors more severely than MAE.
  • R² (Coefficient of Determination): \(1 - \frac{SS_{\text{res}}}{SS_{\text{tot}}}\). Measures the proportion of total sales variance explained by the model compared to a naive mean predictor. An \(R^2 > 0.85\) indicates strong predictive power.

9. Future Scope & Advanced Deep Learning Roadmap

In future enterprise iterations, StyleSense can be augmented with specialized architectures:

  • Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs / Vision Transformers): Extract latent fashion embeddings directly from apparel catalog photos (sleeve length, silhouette, neckline, floral prints) to predict aesthetic trend popularity before launch.
  • Large Language Models (LLMs): Scrape and synthesize thousands of customer reviews and social media comments to capture real-time customer sentiment and emerging aesthetic micro-trends.
  • Recurrent Architectures (LSTM / Temporal Fusion Transformers): Model sequential time-series autocorrelation, multi-week marketing lag effects, and product cannibalization across weeks.

10. Limitations & Ethical Considerations

  • Synthetic Heuristics: While generated with retail econometric formulas, synthetic data cannot replicate real-world black-swan events (supply chain disruptions, viral social media crises, macroeconomic recessions).
  • In-Browser Footprint: TensorFlow.js runs in WebGL/CPU inside the browser, optimized for lightweight demonstrations rather than multi-billion parameter enterprise pipelines.
  • Interpretability Trade-off: Unlike linear regression where weights directly provide marginal dollar elasticity per rupee of ad spend, neural networks act as non-linear black boxes, necessitating post-hoc explainability tools (like SHAP values) in corporate settings.
